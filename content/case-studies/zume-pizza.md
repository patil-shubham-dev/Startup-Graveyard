## The Story — How it started, founding story, vision

Zume Pizza was founded in 2015 by Alex Garden, a former Microsoft executive, and a team of entrepreneurs. The company's vision was to revolutionize the pizza delivery industry by introducing robotic delivery systems. The founders aimed to create a seamless, efficient, and cost-effective experience for customers, leveraging cutting-edge technology to streamline the delivery process.

The initial idea was to use a network of autonomous vehicles to transport pizzas from local pizzerias to customers' doorsteps. This concept was touted as a game-changer in the industry, promising to reduce labor costs, increase delivery speed, and enhance customer satisfaction. The founders envisioned a future where robots would become an integral part of the pizza delivery ecosystem.

Zume Pizza's founding story was marked by a sense of excitement and optimism. The company secured its first funding round of $3.5 million in 2015, which was used to develop the initial prototype of the robotic delivery system. The founders' vision was to create a scalable business model that would disrupt the traditional pizza delivery industry.

## The Rise — Growth, funding, traction

Between 2016 and 2018, Zume Pizza experienced a period of rapid growth, securing multiple funding rounds that brought the company's valuation to over $200 million. The company's robotic delivery system was showcased at various industry events, generating significant media attention and public interest.

Zume Pizza partnered with several major pizza chains, including Domino's and Pizza Hut, to integrate its robotic delivery system into their operations. The company's technology was touted as a solution to the industry's labor shortages and increasing delivery costs.

However, despite the initial hype and growth, Zume Pizza struggled to achieve significant market traction. The company's robotic delivery system faced numerous technical challenges, including navigation, mapping, and sensor issues. These problems hindered the system's ability to operate efficiently and reliably, leading to delays and cancellations.

Several warning signs emerged early on in Zume Pizza's development, but the company's leadership and investors chose to overlook or downplay these concerns. One of the primary issues was the company's high operational costs, which were driven by the development and maintenance of the robotic delivery system.

The company's reliance on expensive hardware and software solutions made it difficult to achieve economies of scale, leading to high costs per delivery. Additionally, Zume Pizza's business model was heavily dependent on partnerships with major pizza chains, which limited the company's ability to innovate and differentiate itself.

Another critical issue was the lack of market need for Zume Pizza's solution. While the company's founders believed that their technology would revolutionize the pizza delivery industry, many experts questioned whether the market was truly ready for such a disruption.
