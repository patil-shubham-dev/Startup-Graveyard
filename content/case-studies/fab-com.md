## Background
The e-commerce industry was rapidly growing in the early 2010s, driven by the increasing adoption of smartphones and the rise of social media. Online marketplaces like Amazon and eBay were already established, but there was a growing demand for platforms that connected consumers directly with designers and makers. This shift in consumer behavior created an opportunity for companies that could offer unique products and personalized shopping experiences.

### Market Landscape
The social commerce space was gaining traction, with companies like Pinterest and Etsy gaining popularity. However, these platforms focused on specific niches, such as visual discovery and handmade goods, respectively. The market was ripe for a platform that could aggregate a wide range of products from various designers and makers, creating a one-stop-shop for consumers.

### Industry Trends
Several trends contributed to the viability of Fab.com's business model:

* The rise of social media and online communities, which facilitated the discovery of new products and designers
* The increasing demand for unique, handmade, and sustainable products
* The growth of the maker movement, which emphasized the importance of craftsmanship and community involvement
* The proliferation of e-commerce platforms and payment gateways, making it easier for businesses to sell online

## Founding Story
Fab.com was founded in 2010 by Jason Goldberg, a seasoned entrepreneur and e-commerce expert. Goldberg had previously founded other successful companies, including Jobster and Socializr. He saw an opportunity to create a platform that would connect consumers with designers and makers, providing a unique shopping experience that combined social features with e-commerce functionality.

### Founding Team
Goldberg assembled a team of experienced professionals, including:

* Bradford Shellhammer, a well-known designer and entrepreneur, who joined as the company's creative director
* Adam Pritzker, a seasoned investor and entrepreneur, who joined as the company's chairman
* Jason Goldberg's wife, Susan, who served as the company's CEO

### Original Thesis
Fab.com's original thesis was to create a platform that would allow designers and makers to showcase and sell their products directly to consumers, while also providing a social experience that would facilitate community engagement and discovery.

## Product Development
Fab.com's product development process was iterative and involved significant changes over time. The company's initial product was a social commerce platform that allowed designers and makers to create virtual storefronts and sell their products to consumers.

### Key Features
The platform's key features included:

* A social feed that allowed users to discover and interact with products
* A marketplace where designers and makers could sell their products
* A community feature that facilitated user engagement and feedback

### Technology Choices
The company chose to build its platform using a combination of open-source and proprietary technologies, including Ruby on Rails and MongoDB.

### Development Approach
Fab.com's development approach was agile, with a focus on rapid iteration and continuous improvement.

## Launch & Go-to-Market
Fab.com launched in 2011, with a focus on creating a buzz around the platform. The company's marketing strategy included:

* A $1 million marketing campaign that targeted designers and makers
* A social media campaign that emphasized the platform's unique features
* A series of events and contests that encouraged user engagement

### Initial Reception
The platform received positive reviews from users and critics alike, with many praising its unique features and user-friendly interface.

### Early Customers
Fab.com's early customers included a mix of established designers and makers, as well as up-and-coming talent.

### Pricing
The platform charged a commission on sales, ranging from 10% to 20% depending on the product category.

## Growth & Traction
Fab.com experienced rapid growth in its early years, with user adoption and revenue increasing steadily.

### Key Growth Metrics
* User acquisition costs decreased from $20 to $5 per user
* Average order value increased from $20 to $50
* Revenue growth rate exceeded 100% YoY for several quarters

### Funding Rounds
The company raised several rounds of funding, including a $150 million Series C round in 2012.

### Expansion into New Markets
Fab.com expanded into new markets, including fashion and home decor, and launched a series of initiatives to support emerging designers and makers.

## Challenges & Strategic Decisions
Despite its early success, Fab.com faced significant challenges, including:

* Increasing competition from established e-commerce platforms
* Difficulty in achieving profitability
* Leadership changes and internal conflicts

### Major Obstacles
* The company struggled to achieve profitability, with losses exceeding $100 million in 2013
* The platform faced significant competition from established e-commerce players, including Amazon and eBay
* Internal conflicts and leadership changes hindered the company's ability to execute its strategy

### Strategic Pivots
* The company shifted its focus from social commerce to e-commerce, with a greater emphasis on product discovery and sales
* Fab.com launched a series of initiatives to support emerging designers and makers, including a mentorship program and a funding competition

## Decline
Fab.com's decline began in 2013, with the company missing its revenue targets and struggling to achieve profitability.

### Missed Targets
* The company missed its revenue targets by 20% in 2013
* The platform's user growth rate slowed significantly, with user acquisition costs increasing

### Failed Experiments
* The company launched a series of failed experiments, including a mobile app and a social media platform

### Cash Runway Problems
* Fab.com's cash runway problems became increasingly severe, with the company burning through $100 million in 2014

### Leadership Changes
* The company underwent significant leadership changes, including the departure of its CEO and several other key executives

## Shutdown
Fab.com shut down in 2015, with the company selling its assets and laying off its staff.

### Asset Sales
* The company sold its assets, including its platform and intellectual property, to a series of buyers
* Fab.com's technology was spun out into a new company, which continued to develop and market the platform

### Layoffs
* The company laid off its staff, with many employees departing the company without severance packages

### Founder Statements
* Jason Goldberg, Fab.com's founder, stated that the company's failure was due to its inability to achieve profitability and its failure to execute its strategy

## Legacy
Fab.com's legacy includes:

* The development of a unique social commerce platform that combined social features with e-commerce functionality
* The creation of a community of designers and makers who continue to use and develop the platform
* Lessons absorbed by the industry, including the importance of achieving product-market fit and the need for agile development and continuous improvement.
