## The Story — How it started, founding story, vision

Better.com was founded in 2016 by Vishal Garg, a former Wall Street executive. The company's initial vision was to disrupt the traditional mortgage refinancing and home equity loan industry by offering a streamlined, online application process. Garg's goal was to make it easier for homeowners to access affordable financing options.

Better.com's early success was fueled by a strong team of experienced professionals and a robust technology platform. The company secured $200 million in funding from prominent investors, including SoftBank and Kleiner Perkins. As the company grew, it expanded its offerings to include a range of financial products, including home equity loans and lines of credit.

### Founding Team

*   Vishal Garg: Founder and CEO
*   Kevin Ryan: Co-Founder and President
*   Jay Faris: Co-Founder and CTO

### Early Funding

*   $200 million in Series A funding from SoftBank and Kleiner Perkins
*   $100 million in Series B funding from Tiger Global Management

## The Rise — Growth, funding, traction

Better.com experienced rapid growth in the early years, with the company's valuation reaching $7 billion in 2021. The company's online application process and streamlined approval process made it easier for homeowners to access affordable financing options.

### Key Milestones

*   2016: Better.com is founded by Vishal Garg and Kevin Ryan
*   2017: Company secures $200 million in Series A funding
*   2018: Company expands offerings to include home equity loans and lines of credit
*   2020: Company secures $100 million in Series B funding
*   2021: Company's valuation reaches $7 billion

### Marketing and Branding

*   Company invested heavily in digital marketing and branding efforts
*   Social media campaigns and influencer partnerships helped to raise awareness and drive engagement

## Warning Signs — Early red flags, missed signals

Despite the company's rapid growth and success, there were early warning signs that the company was struggling. These signs were largely ignored or downplayed by the company's leadership.

### Early Red Flags

*   High employee turnover rates
*   Poor customer service ratings
*   Regulatory issues and fines
*   Lack of transparency in company finances

### Missed Signals

*   Failure to address employee concerns and complaints
*   Inadequate training and support for new hires
*   Insufficient resources and budget for customer service and support

## The Collapse — The shutdown, what happened

In March 2022, Better.com announced that it would be laying off 900 employees, approximately 9% of its workforce. The layoffs were a result of the company's failure to secure additional funding and its struggles to remain profitable.

### Layoffs and Restructuring

*   900 employees laid off, approximately 9% of workforce
*   Company undergoes significant restructuring efforts
*   CEO Vishal Garg steps down as CEO, but remains on company's board

### Bankruptcy and Liquidation

*   Company files for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection
*   Assets are sold off to pay creditors
*   Company's intellectual property and technology platform are acquired by a rival company

## Aftermath — Lessons learned, legacy

The collapse of Better.com serves as a cautionary tale for startups and established companies alike. The company's failure to address early warning signs and its struggles to remain profitable ultimately led to its downfall.

### Lessons Learned

*   Importance of prioritizing employee well-being and satisfaction
*   Need for transparency and open communication in company finances and operations
*   Importance of regulatory compliance and adherence to industry standards
