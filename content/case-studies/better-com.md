## Background

The US mortgage and banking industry in the early 2010s was characterized by a lack of innovation and high customer dissatisfaction. Traditional lenders were slow to adapt to changing market conditions, and customers faced lengthy and often frustrating application processes. The rise of fintech companies, which leveraged technology to provide more efficient and customer-centric services, created an opportunity for disruption.

The industry landscape was marked by a growing demand for digital mortgage solutions, driven by the increasing popularity of online banking and the need for faster and more convenient loan processing. Key trends included the growing adoption of digital mortgage platforms, the increasing use of big data and analytics to improve underwriting and risk assessment, and the emergence of new players in the market, such as online lenders and fintech startups.

## Founding Story

Better.com was founded in 2016 by Vishal Garg, a former Wall Street executive with a background in finance and technology. Garg's vision was to create a digital mortgage platform that would provide a faster, more efficient, and more customer-centric alternative to traditional lenders. The company's original thesis was that the mortgage industry was ripe for disruption, and that a well-designed digital platform could capture a significant share of the market.

Garg assembled a team of experienced finance and technology professionals, and the company began to develop its platform. The initial product was designed to provide a streamlined online application process, automated underwriting, and faster loan processing times. The company's early focus was on the US market, where the demand for digital mortgage solutions was highest.

## Product Development

Better.com's initial product was a digital mortgage platform that allowed customers to apply for loans online, receive instant pre-approvals, and track the status of their applications in real-time. The platform used a combination of machine learning algorithms and human underwriters to assess creditworthiness and make lending decisions. The company's technology stack included a proprietary loan origination system (LOS), a customer relationship management (CRM) system, and a range of third-party integrations with data providers and other financial institutions.

The platform was designed to be highly scalable and flexible, with a modular architecture that allowed for easy integration with new features and technologies. The company's development approach was agile, with a focus on rapid prototyping and continuous iteration. This allowed the company to quickly respond to changing market conditions and customer feedback.
