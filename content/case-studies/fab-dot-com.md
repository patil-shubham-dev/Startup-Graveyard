## The Story — How it started, founding story, vision

Fab.com was founded in 2010 by Bradford Shellhammer, a well-known designer and entrepreneur. The company's vision was to create a social e-commerce platform that would allow users to discover and purchase unique, high-quality products from around the world.

Initially, Fab.com focused on selling high-end home decor and furniture items, targeting a niche market of design-conscious consumers. The company's early success was fueled by its unique approach to e-commerce, which emphasized storytelling and community engagement.

However, as the company grew, its vision and mission began to shift. Fab.com started to expand its product offerings to include a wide range of categories, from fashion to electronics. This expansion led to a dilution of the company's brand identity and a loss of focus on its core values.

## The Rise — Growth, funding, traction

In 2011, Fab.com secured $40 million in funding from investors, including Andreessen Horowitz and Founders Fund. This influx of capital enabled the company to aggressively expand its operations and marketing efforts.

During this period, Fab.com experienced rapid growth, with sales increasing by over 1,000% in just a few months. The company's social media presence also grew significantly, with millions of followers on platforms like Facebook and Twitter.

However, beneath the surface, Fab.com was struggling to find a sustainable business model. The company's reliance on venture capital funding created a culture of extravagance and waste, with lavish spending on marketing and employee perks.

## Warning Signs — Early red flags, missed signals

Despite its rapid growth, Fab.com was plagued by early warning signs of trouble. In 2012, the company's CEO, Jason Goldberg, departed abruptly, citing creative differences with the company's founder.

Additionally, Fab.com's financials began to show signs of strain. The company's burn rate was unsustainable, with losses mounting to millions of dollars per quarter. However, investors and management failed to address these issues, instead choosing to focus on short-term growth and market share.

As the company continued to expand, it became clear that Fab.com was struggling to find a viable business model. The company's product offerings were becoming increasingly commoditized, and its marketing efforts were failing to drive meaningful sales.

## The Collapse — The shutdown, what happened

In 2015, Fab.com's investors finally pulled the plug, shutting down the company and laying off over 400 employees. The shutdown was a result of the company's failure to find a sustainable business model, combined with its unsustainable burn rate and lack of traction in the market.

In the aftermath of the shutdown, it became clear that Fab.com had been operating on a fundamentally flawed business model. The company's reliance on venture capital funding had created a culture of extravagance and waste, and its failure to find a viable business model had ultimately led to its downfall.

## Aftermath — Lessons learned, legacy

The failure of Fab.com serves as a cautionary tale for entrepreneurs and investors alike. The company's story highlights the dangers of relying on venture capital funding to fuel growth, and the importance of finding a sustainable business model.

Despite its failure, Fab.com's legacy lives on in the form of its innovative approach to e-commerce and community engagement. The company's focus on storytelling and social media marketing helped to establish a new standard for online retail, and its influence can still be seen in the e-commerce platforms of today.

However, the failure of Fab.com also serves as a reminder of the importance of discipline and focus in business. The company's failure to find a viable business model and its unsustainable burn rate ultimately led to its downfall, and serve as a warning to entrepreneurs and investors to prioritize long-term sustainability over short-term growth.

In the end, the story of Fab.com is one of hubris and failure, but also of innovation and perseverance. Despite its ultimate demise, the company's legacy continues to inspire and educate entrepreneurs and investors, serving as a reminder of the importance of discipline, focus, and sustainability in business.
