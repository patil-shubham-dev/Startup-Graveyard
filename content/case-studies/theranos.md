## The Story

### Founding and Vision

Theranos was founded in 2003 by Elizabeth Holmes, a then-19-year-old Stanford University dropout. Holmes' vision was to revolutionize the healthcare industry by developing a portable, point-of-care blood testing device that could perform multiple tests with a single drop of blood. The company's early pitch emphasized the potential for widespread adoption and cost savings.

### Initial Traction

Theranos gained initial traction in 2005 when it secured its first $500,000 in funding from investors, including Tim Draper and Draper Fisher Jurvetson. The company's early success was fueled by Holmes' charisma and the promise of its proprietary miniLab technology. Theranos' early product, the Edison, was touted as a game-changer in the healthcare industry.

### Early Partnerships

Theranos formed partnerships with several major healthcare companies, including Walgreens and Safeway, to integrate its technology into their stores. The partnerships were seen as a major coup for the company, and they helped to fuel its growth. By 2010, Theranos had raised over $400 million in funding and had partnerships with over 200 healthcare providers.

## The Rise

### Key Funding Rounds

Theranos secured several major funding rounds, including a $100 million investment from Larry Ellison in 2013. The funding round valued the company at $9 billion, making Holmes one of the youngest female billionaires in the world. Theranos also raised $350 million in a 2014 funding round, which was led by investors such as Larry Ellison and Tim Draper.

### Expansion and Peak Metrics

Theranos expanded rapidly, opening over 40 centers in the United States and abroad. The company's peak metrics included over 1,000 employees, $1 billion in annual revenue, and a valuation of over $9 billion. Theranos' technology was used by over 1 million patients, and the company had partnerships with several major healthcare companies.

### Regulatory Issues

Despite its rapid growth, Theranos faced several regulatory issues. In 2015, the company was sued by the Arizona Attorney General's office for allegedly making false claims about its technology. Theranos was also under investigation by the FDA and the SEC.

## Warning Signs

### Early Red Flags

Several early red flags were ignored by Theranos' investors and executives. In 2010, a former Theranos employee raised concerns about the company's testing procedures, but the concerns were dismissed by Holmes. In 2013, a Wall Street Journal investigation revealed that Theranos' technology was not as advanced as claimed, but the company's investors and executives continued to tout its potential.

### Missed Signals

Several missed signals foreshadowed the end of Theranos. In 2014, the company's board of directors was informed of several issues with its testing procedures, but the concerns were not acted upon. In 2015, a former Theranos employee came forward with allegations of misconduct, but the company's investors and executives continued to support Holmes.

### Strategic Mistakes

Several strategic mistakes contributed to Theranos' downfall. The company's over-reliance on its proprietary miniLab technology made it vulnerable to regulatory issues and competition. Theranos' aggressive expansion and blitzscaling strategy also led to cash exhaustion and a lack of product-market fit.

## The Collapse

### Shutdown Event

Theranos shut down in 2018 after a series of regulatory issues and a massive scandal. The company's investors and executives had lost confidence in Holmes and the company's leadership. In the final months, Theranos' employees were told to destroy their laptops and other company property, and the company's assets were sold off.

### Key Decisions

Several key decisions sealed the fate of Theranos. In 2017, the company's board of directors was informed of several issues with its testing procedures, but the concerns were not acted upon. In 2018, Holmes and her COO, Sunny Balwani, were charged with wire fraud and conspiracy by the SEC.

## Aftermath

### Lessons Learned

Theranos' failure serves as a cautionary tale for healthcare startups. The company's over-reliance on its proprietary technology and aggressive expansion strategy led to cash exhaustion and a lack of product-market fit. The failure also highlights the importance of regulatory compliance and transparency in the healthcare industry.

### Legacy

Theranos' legacy is one of cautionary tales and regulatory failures. The company's failure led to a massive scandal and the downfall of its charismatic CEO, Elizabeth Holmes. However, the company's failure also led to increased scrutiny of healthcare startups and the importance of regulatory compliance and transparency.

### What Could Have Been Different

Several things could have been different to prevent Theranos' downfall. The company's investors and executives could have been more skeptical of its claims and more vigilant in monitoring its regulatory compliance. The company could have also taken a more cautious approach to expansion and product development.
